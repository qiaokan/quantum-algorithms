A complex number stores two real coordinates: $z=a+bi$. The symbol $i$ is defined by $i^2=-1$; $a$ is the real part and $b$ the imaginary part. Complex amplitudes let rotations carry more information than a positive/negative sign.

Multiply by distributing ordinary products: $(1+2i)(3-i)=3-i+6i-2i^2=5+5i$. The conjugate changes the imaginary sign: $\overline z=a-bi$. Its squared magnitude is $|z|^2=z\overline z=a^2+b^2$. Thus $|(1+i)/2|^2=(1^2+1^2)/4=1/2$.

The expression $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ means a point on the unit circle at angle $\theta$, in radians. One full turn is $2\pi$. Consequently $e^{i\pi}=-1$ and $e^{i\pi/2}=i$. Multiplication adds angles. See [trigonometry](/definitions/trigonometry/) for the circle calculation.

**Predict:** Does multiplying an amplitude by $i$ change its probability? No: $|iz|^2=|i|^2|z|^2=|z|^2$. Its phase changes; later additions can reveal that change. Return to the parent expression and square magnitudes only after adding contributions to each outcome.
